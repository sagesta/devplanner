# Using DevPlanner day to day

Start with **Add task → Today → Done**. A task title is enough. You do not need a goal, weekly plan, AI account, or connected calendar.

## Capture and choose

Use Add task to record one item. Choose Inbox when it is not for today. Add multiple tasks opens multiline capture; AI organization is optional and its suggestions remain editable before saving. Close preserves your device's draft; Discard explicitly clears it. Failed saves retain the draft for retry.

Today shows your current work and upcoming tasks. Pick from Inbox stays available even when the day already contains tasks. Tasks with smaller steps let you choose the steps you intend to do. General is the automatic area for uncategorized tasks; choose another area later in details.

## Change your day

Select a title to open its details. Do next changes your next choice without moving calendar times or changing priority. Tomorrow and Choose date move planned work while leaving its deadline intact. Open deadline reminders separately if a postponed task is still due. Show all reveals the rest of a long agenda; an energy filter does not mean hidden work is complete.

Timers are optional. If another timer is running, resolve it explicitly when switching. Completing one step should not silently complete its siblings or stop timing unrelated work. A save is confirmed only after the server accepts it.

## Plan and reflect when useful

This week, Board, Timeline and Table are alternative planning views. Goals provide optional direction. Weekly review can be completed without creating another week of work; choose that option only when wanted.

If another device changed a review, keep your local text and compare versions before replacing it. Drafts are bound to your signed-in account and review week. A new week does not move unfinished reflection text to the new period automatically.

Set your planning timezone in Settings → General. Today and review boundaries use that setting across devices. Calendar sync failures do not erase committed task changes; the worker retries pending synchronization.
